  photoperiodism and taxis.

  Excellence — link plant flowering cues to pollinator behaviour; discuss experimental evidence, justify
  conclusions from the data.

## 91603-2023-Q1 · 2023 Exam Q1 · Achieved/Merit/Excellence

- Location: 91603-exm-2023.pdf, Question One, tulip responses (abiotic).
- Topic: tropism vs nastic, negative geotropism, positive phototropism, thermonasty and
  photonasty, and how they aid pollination.
  the tulip. In your answer, include discussion of: the terms tropism and nastic, including
  definitions; an advantage for the tulip in exhibiting negative geotropism and an advantage in
  exhibiting positive phototropism; how exhibiting both thermonasty and photonasty helps to
  ensure successful pollination in tulips."
- Answer: Achievement — describes tropism; nastic response; thermonasty or photonasty;
  positive phototropism and its advantage; negative geotropism and its advantage; the need for
  warm conditions for pollinators. Merit — explains the advantage of negative geotropism
  (keeps cut stems upright, holding flower and leaves up), of positive phototropism (growth
  toward light benefits pollination/photosynthesis), of thermonasty and of photonasty
  (non-directional responses to temperature and to light that open the flower only when
  pollination is likely). Excellence — discusses how nastic and tropic responses work in sync
  so flowers open in warm, sunny or windy conditions when pollinators are active, and links
  increased pollination to greater reproductive success, increased variation and a larger
  population.
- Tier/marks: ladder N0/N1/N2, A3/A4 (1–4 Achievement evidence points), M5/M6 (2–3
  Merit points), E7/E8 (1–2 Excellence points), cut scores 0–8 / 9–13 / 14–18 / 19–24
  (91603-ass-2023.pdf pp1–2).
- Verified: question verified in 91603-exm-2023.pdf Q1; model answer, three tier columns

## 91603-2023-Q2 · 2023 Exam Q2 · Achieved/Merit/Excellence

- Location: 91603-exm-2023.pdf, Question Two, Chillingham wild cattle.
- Topic: dominance hierarchy, ritualised vs full-contact fighting, intraspecific competition.
  answer, include discussion of: dominance hierarchy, including a definition; an advantage of
  the hierarchy for the king; an advantage for the females in having a king in the herd; why
  fighting is usually ritualised but may escalate to a physical fight at times."
- Answer: Achievement — describes a hierarchy (organisation by rank/dominance, set by age,
  sex or genetic fitness); ritualised behaviours; an advantage to the king; an advantage to the
  females; intraspecific competition; when full-contact fighting may occur. Merit — explains
  how the hierarchy increases the king's chances of success (mates with all females, priority
  access to food, not challenged), how it helps the females (protected, assured of breeding
  with fit genes), how intraspecific competition means only the fittest reproduce, why
  ritualised fighting avoids injury, how the gene pool stays healthy, and why escalation
  happens (limited resource, a lower-ranked bull challenging for rank). Excellence — discusses
  hierarchical behaviour, both ritualised and full-contact: ritualising saves energy for
  reproduction and prevents injury, while full-contact fighting can cause injury or death,
  reducing herd size and genetic variation.
- Tier/marks: same N0–E8 ladder counting Achievement / Merit / Excellence evidence points
  (91603-ass-2023.pdf p3).
- Verified: question verified in 91603-exm-2023.pdf Q2; model answer and all three tier
  columns read directly from 91603-ass-2023.pdf Question Two. HIGH.
