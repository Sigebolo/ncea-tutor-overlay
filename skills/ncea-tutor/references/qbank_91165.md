## 91165-2022-Q1aiii · 2022 Exam Q1(a)(iii) · Achieved/Merit

- Location: 91165-exm-2022.pdf, Question One (a)(iii), isomerism B vs C.
- Topic: constitutional vs geometric isomerism.
  Compounds B and C... Explain your choice."
- Answer: constitutional (structural) isomerism — same molecular formula

  (so geometric isomerism is ruled out).

  geometric, Merit = type + explanation linked to structures. HIGH.

## 91165-2024-Q1b · 2024 Exam Q1(b) · Achieved/Merit/Excellence

- Location: 91165-exm-2024.pdf, Question One (b), three colourless solvents.
- Topic: distinguishing methanol, ethanol and hexane using only melting point, boiling point
  and water solubility.
  ethanol, and hexane, based solely on their physical properties. Give reasons for the
  different results between solvents. Physical properties are limited to differences in melting
  point, boiling point, and solubility in water."
- Answer: Add water to each unknown. Methanol and ethanol are miscible (short carbon
  chain → one layer); hexane is immiscible → two layers. Then take a boiling point of the two
  alcohols: ethanol boils higher than methanol because it has the longer carbon chain /
  greater mass.
- Tier/marks: Achievement — identifies ethanol or methanol as alcohols, OR hexane as an
  alkane; Merit — ONE correct method with its observation; Excellence — identifies hexane by
  solubility with an explanation OR states ethanol/methanol differ in boiling point, and
  explains how all three are told apart with reference to carbon chain length.
- Verified: question verified in 91165-exm-2024.pdf Q1(b); procedure, answer and all three
  tier bullets read directly from 91165-ass-2024.pdf Q1 (b), page 2. HIGH.

## 91165-2024-Q1cii · 2024 Exam Q1(c)(ii) · Achieved/Merit/Excellence

- Location: 91165-exm-2024.pdf, Question One (c)(ii), styrene vs polystyrene.
- Topic: addition polymerisation, C=C vs C–C, and how structure controls reactivity and use.
  reactive than the polymer polystyrene. In your answer you should: explain the term
  'addition polymerisation'; explain the difference in structure, and link it to chemical
  reactivity; relate how this difference is important for the uses of the polymer."
- Answer: In addition polymerisation the C=C double bonds break so new single bonds form
  between monomers, joining them into long repeating chains. The monomer's C=C
  unsaturated double bond is much more reactive than the polymer's saturated C–C single
  bond. That stability is what makes polystyrene suitable for food containers: plates, bowls,
  cutlery and bags do not react with food, moisture or heat.
- Tier/marks: Achievement — identifies the polymer as saturated (C–C) or the monomer as
  unsaturated (C=C), OR states why low reactivity is good for the products; Merit — partial
  explanation of addition polymerisation, and links the saturated/unsaturated nature to
  reactivity; Excellence — explains addition polymerisation and the monomer/polymer
  difference with reference to structure, reactivity and uses.
- Verified: question verified in 91165-exm-2024.pdf Q1(c)(ii); answer and tier bullets read
  directly from 91165-ass-2024.pdf Q1 (c)(ii), page 2. HIGH.
