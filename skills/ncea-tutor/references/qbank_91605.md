## 91605-2024-Q1 · 2024 Exam Q1 · Achieved/Merit/Excellence

- Location: 91605-exm-2024.pdf, Question One (Kōura).
- Topic: freshwater crayfish kōura; small inbreeding populations; Māori translocation (gene flow); three mtDNA
  groups across the map.
- Answer: Achievement — founder population: a small subset breaks away to colonise a new area, with a different
  (narrower) gene pool; gene flow (movement of alleles between populations) keeps populations from diverging;
  limited gene flow → divergence; Southern Alps (~5 mya, Indo-Australian + Pacific plate boundary, orogeny)
  created geographical barriers that split populations.
  Merit — explain how isolation + founder effect + natural selection produced the three mtDNA groups.
  Excellence — link genetic data to tectonic history, discuss why translocation aids each population's
  survival (restores variation), justify management.
- Tier/marks: N0/N1/N2, A3/A4, M5/M6, E7/E8 (cut scores 0–7 NA, 8–13 A, 14–18 M, 19–24 E per 91605-ass-2024.pdf).
- Verified: matches 91605-ass-2024.pdf p2 (founder populations; gene flow; orogeny/barriers). HIGH.

- Topic: tammar and parma wallabies allopatric in Australia, sympatric on Kawau Island; phylogeny of adaptive

- Answer: Achievement — sympatric = closely related species in the same geographical area (different niche);

  Excellence — link to adaptive radiation of the wallaby phylogeny, discuss selection pressures and isolation

## 91605-2024-Q3 · 2024 Exam Q3 · Achieved/Merit/Excellence

- Location: 91605-exm-2024.pdf, Question Three (A case of convergent evolution).
- Topic: flight evolved independently in many groups — honeybee (non-bone wing) vs bird wing; same function,
  different structure; discuss convergent evolution and selection pressures.
- Answer: Achievement — convergent evolution: distantly related species become morphologically similar over time
  via natural selection; selection pressures = environmental factors (abiotic: wind/temperature; biotic: mate
  selection/predation) that favour survival + reproduction; similar features appear because similar pressures.
  Merit — explain how the demanding physics of flight selects similar wing/attachment solutions; how
  unrelated lineages reach analogous structure.
  Excellence — link genetic (distant) vs morphological (similar) evidence, discuss how similar selection
  pressures drive parallel form, justify using flight as a classic example.
- Tier/marks: N0/N1/N2, A3/A4, M5/M6, E7/E8 (cut scores as above per 91605-ass-2024.pdf).
- Verified: matches 91605-ass-2024.pdf p4 (convergent evolution; selection pressures). HIGH.

## 91605-2023-Q1 · 2023 Exam Q1 · Achieved/Merit/Excellence

- Location: 91605-exm-2023.pdf, Question One, kākāpō genetic diversity.
- Topic: mutation, gene pool, founder effect, genetic drift, natural selection, future of the
  population.
  populations. In your answer, include discussion of: the terms mutation and gene pool,
  including definitions; how the founder effect enables genetic drift to be more apparent in the
  kākāpō species; how genetic drift and natural selection may have implications for the survival
  of the species."
- Answer: Achievement — defines mutation (random, permanent change in the base sequence
  of DNA) and gene pool (all the alleles within a population); describes a founder population,
  genetic drift, natural selection, a selection pressure (disease, predation, habitat loss), and a
  plausible future implication (extinction / further decline). Merit — explains why founder
  effect makes drift more visible (small numbers), how natural selection removes harmful
  mutations in the small island population (fewer than in the mainland population), how drift
  fixes or loses alleles, and implications for the future (loss of genetic diversity / smaller gene
  pool; speciation or environmental change bringing new selection pressures and possible
  extinction). Excellence — discusses drift, mutation and natural selection linked together
  across the two kākāpō gene pools, and discusses future implications of both processes (low
  fitness, smaller population, more inbreeding).
- Tier/marks: ladder N0–E8 counting evidence points (1–4 Achievement, 2–3 Merit, 1–2
  Excellence); cut scores 0–6 / 7–12 / 13–18 / 19–24 (91605-ass-2023.pdf pp1–2).
- Verified: question verified in 91605-exm-2023.pdf Q1; model answer, all three tier columns

## 91605-2023-Q2 · 2023 Exam Q2 · Achieved/Merit/Excellence

- Location: 91605-exm-2023.pdf, Question Two, Coprosma adaptive radiation.
- Topic: adaptive radiation and niche, habitat-driven divergence, speciation across islands.
  discussion of: adaptive radiation and niche, including definitions; how different habitats have
  led to adaptive radiation of Coprosma; why Coprosma growing on different islands might have
  different appearances and can be classed as different species."
- Answer: Achievement — defines niche (role of the organism within the environment), species
  (organisms that can interbreed and produce fertile offspring) and adaptive radiation (many
  species from one ancestor in a short time); describes how Coprosma reached multiple
  environments by mutation, a habitat selection pressure (e.g. high altitude in Tasmania =
  more intense sunlight), dispersal to different areas (e.g. by birds), and form linked to
  environment (dryer soils → more extensive root system). Merit — explains adaptive radiation
  through open/vacant niches, how multiple species arise from multiple environments with
  environment-specific features, natural selection pressures (soil/bedrock, climate,
  temperature), dispersal to specific islands (people, birds, ocean currents), and form linked to
  environment. Excellence — discusses radiation across New Zealand and the Pacific with links
  from phenotype to habitat/niche, and how natural selection produces speciation of Coprosma
  after an accumulation of mutations, in a short period of time.
- Tier/marks: same N0–E8 ladder counting evidence points; cut scores 0–6 / 7–12 / 13–18 /
  19–24 (91605-ass-2023.pdf p3).
- Verified: question verified in 91605-exm-2023.pdf Q2; model answer and all three tier
  columns read directly from 91605-ass-2023.pdf Question Two. HIGH.
