## 91606-2024-Q1 · 2024 Exam Q1 · Achieved/Merit/Excellence

- Location: 91606-exm-2024.pdf, Question One (Movement of hominids and hominins).
- Topic: chimpanzee arboreal/quadrupedal + bipedal movement; energy cost of bipedal vs quadrupedal walking in chimps;
  what this suggests (or not) about early hominin habitual bipedalism.
- Answer: Achievement — habitual bipedalism: most locomotion on two/hind legs; bipedalism evidence ~4–5 mya after
  split from the quadrupedal ancestor; advantages: efficient walking over long distances, hands freed to carry,
  better outlook on the savanna, less midday sun, intimidation.
  Merit — explain why the similar chimp energy cost means bipedalism did NOT have to be selected for as an
  energy saving in chimps, but did open niches in hominins.
  Excellence — link skeletal changes to bipedalism, discuss trade-offs, analyse what the chimp data does and
  does not imply for early hominins.
- Tier/marks: N0/N1/N2, A3/A4, M5/M6, E7/E8 (cut scores 0–7 NA, 8–13 A, 14–18 M, 19–24 E per 91606-ass-2024.pdf).
- Verified: matches 91606-ass-2024.pdf p2 (bipedalism timing; advantage list). HIGH.

## 91606-2024-Q3 · 2024 Exam Q3 · Achieved/Merit/Excellence

- Location: 91606-exm-2024.pdf, Question Three (The island of Flores).
- Topic: Homo floresiensis — ~110 cm, small brain, large teeth, large feet; stone tools (50,000–190,000 y),
  hunting small elephants/large rodents; lived with Komodo dragons; did they use fire?
- Answer: Achievement — small size → less daily energy needed; easy hiding/ambushing from Komodo dragon;
  island could support more individuals; large teeth → raw/plant food, no cooking; fish abundant and cheap to
  chew → enough nutrition for the small brain; near-equator long days → no fire needed for light; warm climate
  → no fire needed for heat.
  Merit — explain how island environment (food, predators, day length, temperature) shaped which traits
  mattered; trade-offs of small brain + tools.
  Excellence — link adaptation to selection pressures, discuss the fire-use evidence question, analyse whether
  H. floresiensis fits "small brain = tool/technology" assumptions.
- Tier/marks: N0/N1/N2, A3/A4, M5/M6, E7/E8 (cut scores as above per 91606-ass-2024.pdf).
- Verified: matches 91606-ass-2024.pdf p4 (energy, teeth, food, light, warmth points). HIGH.

## 91606-2023-Q1 · 2023 Exam Q1 · Achieved/Merit/Excellence

- Location: 91606-exm-2023.pdf, Question One, Laetoli footprints.
- Topic: evolution of leg and foot for bipedal locomotion — footprint differences, environment,
  diet.
  bipedal locomotion. In your answer, include discussion of: TWO differences between the
  three footprints, with descriptions; how the environment may have led to changes in the
  structure of the leg and foot, enabling more efficient bipedal locomotion; how being bipedal
  could have led to changes in the diet of early hominins and how these contributed to their
  success."
- Answer: Achievement — identifies bipedal locomotion (habitual walking on two limbs); one
  foot-architecture difference (aligned big toe in humans/Australopithecus vs the chimpanzee's
  side big toe; arch vs flat foot; deeper human heelprint); a second such difference. Describes
  the change to the leg bones from bipedalism, how the stance changed diet, an example of a
  changed diet leading to success, and how the environment changed before bipedalism (cooler,
  drier, forest → savannah, greater distance between resources). Merit — explains each foot
  difference (aligned toe gives push-off and efficiency; arch absorbs shock and allows longer
  foraging; heel-toe walking), the leg-bone change, the environment → bipedalism →
  efficiency link, how diet changed, and biped success through diet (foraging further, carrying
  food to a home base). Excellence — discusses at least two footprint differences (toe, arch,
  heel) with links from climate change to bipedalism to leg change and on to diet/nutrition and
  success, including named dietary changes and survival on the savannah ~5 million years ago.
- Tier/marks: ladder N0–E8 counting evidence points (1–4 Achievement, 2–3 Merit, 2
  Excellence); cut scores 0–7 / 8–13 / 14–18 / 19–24 (91606-ass-2023.pdf pp1–2).
- Verified: question verified in 91606-exm-2023.pdf Q1; model answer, all three tier columns

## 91606-2023-Q2 · 2023 Exam Q2 · Achieved/Merit/Excellence

- Location: 91606-exm-2023.pdf, Question Two, Oldowan to Acheulian tools.
- Topic: how tool use influenced hominin evolution — benefits, power vs precision grip,
  changes in tool culture.
  your answer, include discussion of: TWO benefits resulting from early hominins' use of stone
  tools; an advantage of both the power grip and the precision grip to the use of tools; TWO
  named changes to tools from the Oldowan tool culture to the Acheulian tool culture, and how
  these changes gave advantages to Homo erectus or Homo ergaster."
- Answer: Achievement — describes what a stone tool is (used for a purpose, used repeatedly);
  two benefits of tool use (digging up food, cracking open bones / access to marrow and nuts);
  identifies the power grip (more force) and the precision grip (more precise blows), and that
  Acheulian tools need more blows/planning than Oldowan. Merit — links named tool use to
  increased success; explains benefits (defence with a found tool, specialised tools for better
  food gathering, more food enabling brain growth), the advantage of the precision grip and of
  the power grip to a named use, and how food-gathering success led to brain development and
  species success. Excellence — discusses the development in tool culture with full
  understanding of both Oldowan and Acheulean, connecting grip changes to more
  sophisticated tools, and discusses the positive feedback loop between tools, food and brains
  (better tools → more/better food, e.g. hunting → more protein → larger brains → more
  refined tools, enabling Homo erectus' success).
- Tier/marks: same N0–E8 ladder counting evidence points; cut scores 0–7 / 8–13 / 14–18 /
  19–24 (91606-ass-2023.pdf p3).
- Verified: question verified in 91606-exm-2023.pdf Q2; model answer and all three tier
  columns read directly from 91606-ass-2023.pdf Question Two. HIGH.
